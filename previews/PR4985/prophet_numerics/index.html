PROPHET: Discretization and Time Stepping

The PROPHET equations are continuous. This page gives their discrete form: the spatial discretization, the implicit–explicit split, and the implicit treatment of the draft mass and velocity that makes the scheme run at the dynamical core's timestep rather than at the updraft CFL limit. It also collects the regularizations that keep the scheme well behaved where the drafts are nearly empty or nearly fill the cell.

Two things make the draft equations harder to time-step than the resolved ones. Updraft vertical velocities are large, $O(10)$ m s⁻¹, while the vertical grid spacing near the surface is small, $O(10)$ m, so an explicit treatment of the vertical draft advection would cap the timestep at a few seconds. The draft mass variable $\hat{\rho}^j$ can also approach zero, and the equations divide by it. Both are handled at the time-discretization level.

Spatial discretization

PROPHET uses the same discretization as the dynamical core: a horizontal spectral-element method and vertical finite differences on a Lorenz staggered grid, with the draft vertical velocity $u_3^j$ on faces and everything else at cell centers. The operators, the strong/weak/split-form choices, and the notation used below are defined once in Discretization and Operators; this page uses them without restating them.

Two reconstructed velocities appear, as in the resolved equations:

\[\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}^j = WI^f(\rho J, \boldsymbol{u}_h) + \boldsymbol{u}_v^j , \qquad \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}^j = \boldsymbol{u}_h + I^c(\boldsymbol{u}_v^j) .\]

Note the weight in the face reconstruction. Mass conservation for the draft would call for $\hat{\rho}^j J$, but that weight vanishes with the draft area; the code uses the grid-mean weight $\rho J$, the same weighted interpolation as for the resolved velocity. The horizontal part of the face velocity is therefore shared with the grid mean, and only the vertical covariant component is draft-specific.

The draft kinetic energy at centers is built the same way as the resolved one,

\[\kappa^j = \tfrac{1}{2} \left( \boldsymbol{u}_h \cdot \boldsymbol{u}_h + 2 \boldsymbol{u}_h \cdot I^c(\boldsymbol{u}_v^j) + I^c(\boldsymbol{u}_v^j \cdot \boldsymbol{u}_v^j) \right) ,\]

and the purely vertical part

\[\kappa_v^j = \tfrac{1}{2} \, \boldsymbol{u}_v^j \cdot \boldsymbol{u}_v^j\]

is kept separately at faces (ᶠKᵥʲs), because the vertical advection it generates is treated implicitly and must be excluded from the explicit kinetic-energy gradient.

No-flux boundary conditions are imposed exactly as for the resolved velocity. The third contravariant component of the face velocity is required to vanish at the surface and at the model top, which fixes the covariant component there:

\[u_3^j = - \frac{g^{31} u_1 + g^{32} u_2}{g^{33}} .\]

Draft mass

The flux-form continuity equation is discretized as

\[\frac{\partial \hat{\rho}^j}{\partial t} = - \hat{\mathcal{D}}_h \left[ \hat{\rho}^j \bar{\boldsymbol{u}}^j \right] - D^c \left[ \frac{I^f(\hat{\rho}^j J)}{J^f} \, \tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}^j \right] + \text{sources} ,\]

with the horizontal term explicit (in the code, split_divₕ(ρa ᶜuʲ, 1), which for a unit scalar reduces to the weak divergence) and the vertical term folded into the implicit-stage solve described below.

Draft scalars

All draft scalars are advected in advective form. Horizontally, the code uses the split, skew-symmetric divergence in the combination that makes the advective form exact,

\[- \mathcal{D}^{split}_h(\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}^j, \psi^j) + \psi^j \, \mathcal{D}^{split}_h(\bar{\boldsymbol{u}}^j, 1) ,\]

so that a uniform $\psi^j$ produces no tendency regardless of the divergence of the draft velocity. Vertically, the same construction is applied with the vertical divergence,

\[- D^c \left[ U^f(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}^j, \psi^j) \right] + \psi^j \, D^c \left[ \tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}^j \right] ,\]

so that the reconstruction and the divergence operator are the same ones the flux form would use. The reconstruction $U^f$ is selected by the configuration: edmfx_mse_humidity_upwinding for $h_s^j$, $q_t^j$, and the sedimenting draft microphysics species (default first_order), so that the draft energy and water cross each face with one reconstruction; edmfx_tracer_upwinding for the remaining draft tracers (default first_order); and none for the central reconstruction. The resolvability argument of the high-resolution limit bears on this choice: upwinding adds numerical diffusion to the draft variables, which acts like a residual subgrid flux and so competes with the physical closure as the grid is refined.

The buoyancy source in the energy equation is evaluated at centers from the face-interpolated draft velocity and the normalized density excess,

\[\left[ \frac{\partial h_s^j}{\partial t} \right]_{\mathrm{buoy}} = I^c(\boldsymbol{u}_v^j) \cdot \frac{\rho^j - \rho}{\rho^j} \, G^c[\Phi] .\]

Under non-equilibrium microphysics, updraft_sedimentation! evaluates, for each sedimenting draft condensate or precipitation species $q_\mu^{\sigma j}$, the downward-biased (ᶠtop_bias) sedimentation divergence $S_\mu$ with the lateral-mixing correction where $\partial_z a^j < 0$. The sum $S = \sum_\mu S_\mu$ is the sedimentation source of the draft mass, which the draft-mass Newton residual (sgs_ρa_implicit_tendency!) applies as a rate, and each intensive draft scalar $\chi^j$ receives $(S_\chi - \chi^j S) / \hat{\rho}^j$, with $S_\chi$ its own sedimentation tendency: $S_\mu$ for $q_\mu^{\sigma j}$, $S$ for $q_t^j$, $\sum_\mu S_{h,\mu}$ for $h_s^j$ (the updraft_sedimentation! tendency of $q_\mu^{\sigma j} (h_\mu(T^j) + \Phi)$, with laterally entrained environment condensate at $h_\mu(T^0) + \Phi$), and zero for the other draft tracers.

Draft vertical momentum

Breaking the curl and cross-product terms into horizontal and vertical contributions and dropping the terms that vanish ($\nabla_v \times \boldsymbol{u}_v = 0$) leaves, for the explicit part,

\[\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{u}_v^j}{\partial t} = - \left( C^f[\boldsymbol{u}_h] + \mathcal{C}_h[\boldsymbol{u}_v^j] \right) \times I^f(\boldsymbol{u}_h) - G^f \left[ \kappa^j - I^c(\kappa_v^j) \right] + \text{hyperdiffusion} .\]

Subtracting $I^c(\kappa_v^j)$ removes the purely vertical kinetic-energy gradient, the $\partial_z(w^2/2)$ advection, which the implicit stage solve below carries instead. Buoyancy, the pressure drag, and the entrainment sink are likewise in the implicit solve.

Subgrid-scale fluxes onto the grid mean

The mass-flux contribution is discretized in the difference form of Coupling to the resolved equations: for each subdomain $k$ (drafts and environment), the tendency added to a grid-mean scalar is the vertical transport of $a^k (\chi^k - \chi)$ by the velocity difference,

\[- D^c \left[ \frac{I^f(\rho^k J)}{J^f} \, U^f \!\left( \tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}^k - \tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}, \; a^k (\chi^k - \chi) \right) \right] ,\]

reconstructed with edmfx_sgsflux_upwinding (default none, i.e. central). Writing the flux with both differences taken explicitly makes it vanish identically when $\chi^k = \chi$, whatever the area gradients. For energy, the transported scalar is $h_s^j + \kappa^j$ for the drafts and the grid-mean enthalpy is subtracted; the $q_t$ flux also increments $\rho$, because subgrid moisture transport moves moist air mass.

The diffusive fluxes are evaluated at faces from the face-native diffusivities $K_h$, $K_u$ (see Closures):

  • The turbulent-mixing component $K_h$ acts on the suspended water $q_{t,\mathrm{eff}} = q_t - q_{\mathrm{rai}} - q_{\mathrm{sno}}$ and on the suspended cloud species (ρq_lcl, ρq_icl, ρn_lcl, ρn_ice), which diffuse down their own gradients (species the drafts do not carry, such as the P3 ice number and rime quantities, down their grid-mean gradients); rain and snow receive no turbulent transport, so that rain shafts dominated by sedimentation are not smeared. Energy diffuses through the bracket of Diffusion, $\mathcal{B} = c_p \nabla T + \nabla \Phi + (h_v + \Phi) \nabla q_{t,\mathrm{eff}} + (h_l - h_v) \nabla q_l^{\mathrm{cl}} + (h_i - h_v) \nabla q_i^{\mathrm{cl}}$, with $c_p$ the heat capacity of the diffusing mixture and each thermodynamic coefficient evaluated at centers and interpolated to faces.
  • For PrognosticEDMFX, the grid-mean diffusive flux sums the environment and draft contributions with face weights $w^0 = I^f(\hat{\rho}^0) K_h$ and $w^j = f_j K_h / I^f(1/\max(\hat{\rho}^j, \epsilon))$ ($f_j =$ edmfx_updraft_kh_diffusion_factor), and when edmfx_vertical_diffusion is enabled each draft scalar $\chi^j \in \{h_s^j, q_t^j, q_l^{\mathrm{cl},j}, q_i^{\mathrm{cl},j}, n_l^{\mathrm{cl},j}, n_i^{\mathrm{cl},j}\}$ receives $-\max(\hat{\rho}^j, \epsilon)^{-1} D^c[-w^j G^f[\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}^j]]$, where $q_{t,\mathrm{eff}}^j = q_t^j - q_r^j - q_s^j$ and $h_{s,\mathrm{eff}}^j$ excludes non-diffusing rain and snow enthalpy. The harmonic face mean in $w^j$ is the series conductance of the adjacent cells, so $w^j / \hat{\rho}^j_c \le 2 f_j K_h$ and the intensive tendency is a bounded three-point diffusion of $\chi^j$ for every value of $\hat{\rho}^j_c$ (see PROPHET). When edmfx_vertical_diffusion is false, PrognosticEDMFX reduces to the environment diffusive flux $-D^c[-w^0 G^f[\chi^0]]$.

Hyperdiffusion

Draft variables are hyperdiffused for numerical stability, along terrain-following coordinate surfaces, using the decomposed and unweighted subdomain fields, with the energy and moisture contributions split into sensible-heat, geopotential, and water terms as in the grid mean. See Hyperdiffusion for the operator and the reference-state subtraction.

The implicit–explicit split

PROPHET terms are distributed between the two halves of the IMEX splitting (see Implicit Solver) like this.

Implicit (implicit_tendency!):

  • vertical advection of the draft scalars, the buoyancy source in the draft energy equation, and draft sedimentation (edmfx_sgs_vertical_advection_tendency!);
  • entrainment relaxation of the draft scalars (edmfx_entr_detr_tendency!);
  • the subgrid-scale mass fluxes onto the grid mean (edmfx_sgs_mass_flux_tendency!);
  • the surface mass-source tendency on the draft scalars (edmfx_boundary_condition_tendency!);
  • the cached $u_3^j$ and $\hat{\rho}^j$ stage tendencies (below);
  • the diffusive fluxes, when implicit_diffusion is set.

Explicit (remaining_tendency!):

  • horizontal advection of all draft variables (horizontal_dynamics_tendency! for $\hat{\rho}^j$ and $h_s^j$, horizontal_tracer_advection_tendency! for $q_t^j$ and the draft tracers);
  • the explicit part of the draft vertical momentum equation (vorticity terms and the horizontal/cross kinetic-energy gradient), in explicit_vertical_advection_tendency!;
  • TKE shear and buoyancy production (edmfx_tke_tendency!);
  • hyperdiffusion, the Rayleigh sponge relaxation of the draft scalars toward the grid mean, and the optional horizontal diffusive fluxes;
  • the diffusive fluxes, when implicit_diffusion is not set.

Configurations that use PROPHET normally set implicit_diffusion: true together with approximate_linear_solve_iters: 2, because the diffusive coupling between the grid mean and the drafts is stiff at the timesteps of interest.

Implicit stage for draft mass and velocity

The draft mass $\hat{\rho}^j$ and vertical velocity $u_3^j$ are advanced by the Newton solve together with the other implicit variables. At the start of every implicit stage, initialize_implicit_stage_problem! refreshes the Y-dependent precomputed quantities (set_implicit_precomputed_quantities_core!) so that $\rho^j$ and the buoyancy are those of the stage-entering state, clamps $\hat{\rho}^j$ to $[0, a_{\max} \rho^j]$, so that the solve starts from a feasible area, and overwrites $u_3^j$ with a closed-form column solve of its stage equation (below), which warm-starts the Newton iteration. Within the solve, sgs_u₃_implicit_tendency! and sgs_ρa_implicit_tendency! evaluate the right-hand sides of the $u_3^j$ and $\hat{\rho}^j$ equations at the current Newton iterate, and update_sgs_u₃_and_ρa_jacobian! supplies their Jacobian blocks (see Jacobian).

Vertical velocity

The evolution equation for the physical vertical velocity of a draft, retaining the terms treated implicitly, is

\[\frac{\partial w^j}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \frac{(w^j)^2}{2} = (1 - \alpha_b) \, b^j + E^j (w^0 - w^j) - d^j ,\]

with $\alpha_b$ the virtual-mass coefficient and $d^j$ the form drag of Pressure closure, quadratic in $w^j - w^0$. At an IMEX stage, this becomes an algebraic equation in the new stage value. The environment velocity is eliminated using the mass-flux constraint with the resolved vertical velocity neglected,

\[w^0 - w^j \approx - \frac{\rho}{\hat{\rho}^0} \, w^j ,\]

which is exact for a single draft and is applied unchanged when there are several. Each velocity difference then brings one factor of $\rho / \hat{\rho}^0 = 1/a^0$. The velocity-independent entrainment rates (background, buoyancy-driven, and turbulent), collected in $E^j_0$, contribute the linear sink $E^j_0 w^j / a^0$; the velocity-proportional entrainment $\epsilon^j |w^j - w^0|$ contributes the quadratic sink $\epsilon^j (w^j)^2 / (a^0)^2$, with $\epsilon^j$ the velocity scale that enters the draft-mass equation below, where the rate is evaluated with $w^0 \approx 0$; and the form drag $d^j = a^0 d_c^j (w^j - w^0) |w^j - w^0|$ contributes the quadratic sink $d_c^j (w^j)^2 / a^0$, its weight $a^0$ cancelling one of the two factors from the velocity differences. The area $a^0$ is clamped to $[1 - a_{\max}, 1]$, in both the warm start and the residual, because the stage-entering state or a Newton iterate can carry an area outside that range. The prognostic variable is the covariant component $u_3 = w \Delta z$, so the whole equation carries one factor of $\Delta z$ relative to the equation for $w$. For the warm start (predict_sgs_u₃!), the stage equation at face $i$ is then a quadratic, coupled to the face below through the advection term:

\[\mathcal{A} \, u_3^2 + \mathcal{B} \, u_3 + \mathcal{C} - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{u_3[i-1]}{\Delta z}\right)^2 = 0 .\]

The coefficients $(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}, \mathcal{C})$ (ᶠa, ᶠb, ᶠc in the code) collect the stage term $1/(\gamma \Delta t)$, the entrainment rates, the form drag (only when edmfx_nh_pressure is enabled), the Rayleigh sponge damping rate (only when a sponge is configured), the reduced buoyancy, and the local part of the vertical kinetic-energy advection. The equation is swept upward with Operators.column_accumulate!, each face taking the $+$ root. Clamping the constant term with $\min(0, \cdot)$ keeps the discriminant non-negative and the root non-negative, so the warm start can never return a downward draft velocity.

The Newton residual (sgs_u₃_implicit_tendency!) evaluates the same terms at the iterate: the reduced buoyancy, the vertical kinetic-energy advection upwinded by the sign of $u_3^j$, the linear and quadratic sinks, and the sponge, plus a fixer $-\min(u_3^j, 0)$ that relaxes a negative velocity at $1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$. The tendency is zero at the boundary faces, where $u_3^j$ is held at zero.

Draft mass

The Newton residual for $\hat{\rho}^j$ (sgs_ρa_implicit_tendency!) is the right-hand side of the flux-form equation

\[\frac{\partial \hat{\rho}^j}{\partial t} = - P \, D^c\!\left[ U_1^f(u_3^j, \hat{\rho}^j) \right] + \left( E^j - \Delta^j_* + r \right) \hat{\rho}^j + \frac{F_{\mathrm{sfc}}}{\Delta z} \delta_{i,1} - R ,\]

with $U_1^f$ the first-order upwind face flux selected by the sign of $u^{3,j}$, $\Delta^j_*$ the detrainment without its mass-flux-divergence part, $P$ the detrainment prefactor, $r$ the sedimentation rate, and $R$ the bounds-restoring term, all evaluated at the Newton iterate. The sedimentation mass source of the condensate species enters as the rate (updraft_sedimentation_rate!)

\[r = \frac{1}{\gamma \Delta t} \sum_\mu \Delta q_\mu , \qquad \left( I - \gamma \Delta t \, \hat{\rho}^{-1} \frac{\partial S_\mu}{\partial q_\mu} \right) \Delta q_\mu = \gamma \Delta t \, \hat{\rho}^{-1} S_\mu ,\]

the change of each draft condensate by sedimentation in one linearized backward-Euler step from the current Newton iterate. The flux through each face comes from the cell above, so the operator is upper bidiagonal and each column is solved in one top-to-bottom sweep. For $q_{\mathrm{icl}}$, $q_{\mathrm{rai}}$, and $q_{\mathrm{sno}}$ (mass) and $n_{\mathrm{rai}}$ (2M number), the super-diagonal uses the running column max of the terminal velocity at and above each cell (fill_column_max!) instead of the pointwise $w^n$. This keeps the super-diagonal nonzero at the leading edge of a falling shaft, where the pointwise $w(q^n \approx 0)$ would collapse and pile the whole Newton step into the first empty cell below the shaft; one Newton iteration therefore carries the mass (or number) across multiple empty cells at Courant numbers above one (including Chen2022 cloud-ice fall speeds). The grid-mean sedimentation Jacobian (update_sedimentation_jacobian!) uses the same substitution (including P3 ice rime and ice number) across empty and negative cells, so both solves agree on where the species goes and precipitation arriving from above at Courant numbers above one traverses negative cells in the same stage rather than piling up there. Cloud liquid and $n_{\mathrm{lcl}}$ fall in the Stokes/fixed regime (CFL_sed ≪ 1) and keep the pointwise stencil. The lateral-mixing diagonal keeps the pointwise $w^n$. The residual source is $r \hat{\rho}^j$. The Newton solve takes the same step for the condensate, so the mass the draft gains matches the dilution $\chi^j S / \hat{\rho}^j$ that it applies, also at Courant numbers above one, where the condensate that arrives over the stage differs from the $S$ of the current iterate. Where the draft narrows downward, $S_\mu$ and its derivative are proportional to the local area and $\Delta q_\mu$ does not depend on it, so the rate also holds at the stage area. Under the equilibrium 0-moment microphysics, $r$ is the draft precipitation removal rate (the $q_t^j$ tendency ᶜmp_tendencyʲs.dq_tot_dt).

The residual routes detrainment, the surface source, and the area bound in ways that are easy to misread:

  • Detrainment enters in two ways. The velocity-scale entrainment and the buoyancy-detrainment pieces appear in $(E^j - \Delta^j_*)$. The mass-flux-divergence component of detrainment does not; it becomes the prefactor $P$ on the flux divergence where that divergence is negative, so that it is treated implicitly together with the flux divergence it derives from. Where the draft is at $a_{\max}$, $P$ is zero: the whole converging mass flux is detrained, and none of it is left to grow the area.
  • The surface source enters the first cell. The capped surface mass source $F_{\mathrm{sfc}} / \Delta z$ is added in cell 1 as an area-independent volumetric source. That is the discrete form of the free lower boundary condition on the draft area.
  • The area is bounded by a restoring term and clamps, not by entrainment. The limiters in $(E^j - \Delta^j_*)$ damp rates near the bounds but cannot guarantee them. The residual therefore includes $R = \min(\hat{\rho}^j, 0) + \max(\hat{\rho}^j - \rho^j a_{\max}, 0)$, which relaxes $\hat{\rho}^j$ back into $[0, \rho^j a_{\max}]$ at $1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$; it is a numerical stabilizer, like the negative-velocity fixer on $u_3^j$, not part of the entrainment or detrainment closures. The stage initialization and the state filter (edmfx_filter) clamp $\hat{\rho}^j$ to the same interval. The Jacobian takes the derivative of $R$ as 1 on the bounds as well as outside them: the stage initialization clamps an out-of-bounds stage target onto a bound, and a derivative of 0 there would let the first Newton step move $\hat{\rho}^j$ almost all the way back to the target. The lower bound is zero rather than $\rho^j a_{\min}$, since raising the area to $a_{\min}$ would create draft mass out of nothing; at $a_{\min}$ the lower limiter only switches detrainment off.

Jacobian

The implicit residual is linearized by the Jacobian algorithms of Implicit Solver. For ManualSparseJacobian, the PROPHET blocks are assembled by five update functions that mirror the tendency structure, in a fixed order because they accumulate into shared blocks:

UpdateCovers
update_sgs_advection_jacobian!vertical advection of the draft scalars (assigns the diagonals, including the $-I$ residual term) and draft sedimentation with its lateral-mixing correction and the $T^j$ dependence of its enthalpy flux
update_sgs_diffusion_and_entr_detr_jacobian!vertical diffusion of the draft scalars down their own gradients, and the entrainment relaxation, including the feedback through the environment residual
update_sgs_boundary_condition_jacobian!the surface mass-source relaxation in the first cell, and a Patankar barrier S / (ρʲ a_max − ρ̂ʲ) on the $\hat{\rho}^j$ diagonal that keeps the Newton iterate below $\rho^j a_{\max}$ against the surface mass source S
update_sgs_massflux_jacobian!the mass-flux contributions to the grid-mean scalars
update_sgs_u₃_and_ρa_jacobian!the $u_3^j$ rows (kinetic-energy advection, entrainment and form drag, the sponge, the Patankar barrier against negative buoyancy, and the stable-draft buoyancy feedback; no blocks with respect to the draft scalars) and the $\hat{\rho}^j$ rows (upwinded mass-flux divergence and its $u_3^j$ derivative, net entrainment minus detrainment, the Patankar barrier on the gross gain, and the bounds-restoring term)

The diagonal advection blocks of the draft scalars, $\hat{\rho}^j$, and $u_3^j$ (kinetic energy) advect with $W^j$ instead of the Newton iterate's $w^j$; the off-diagonal derivatives with respect to $u_3^j$ stay at the iterate. $W^j$ (ᶠwʲ_maxs, set by fill_jacobian_updraft_velocity! at each Jacobian update) is swept up the column from zero as

\[W^j_k = \begin{cases} \max\left( w_{\mathrm{pred},k} ,\; W^j_{k-1} \dfrac{C}{1 + C} \right) , & w_{\mathrm{pred},k} > 0 , \\ 0 , & w_{\mathrm{pred},k} = 0 , \end{cases} \qquad C = \frac{W^j_{k-1} \gamma \Delta t}{\Delta z_k} ,\]

from the velocity predicted after a buoyancy-driven step of the $u_3^j$ equation,

\[w_{\mathrm{pred}} = w^+ + \frac{\gamma \Delta t \, b^+} {1 + \gamma \Delta t \left( r_{\mathrm{lin}} + 2 r_{\mathrm{quad}} w^+ \right)} ,\]

with $w^+$ and $b^+$ the positive parts of the iterate's velocity and buoyancy acceleration and $r_{\mathrm{lin}}$, $r_{\mathrm{quad}}$ the linear and quadratic drag rates of the $u_3^j$ equation. $W^j_{k-1} C / (1 + C)$ is the velocity that a backward-Euler upwind step of the kinetic-energy advection carries from the face below into a face at rest, which covers an updraft that rises into empty cells within the step. Nothing is carried across a face where $w_{\mathrm{pred}}$ vanishes, where the iterate is at rest and the buoyancy is not positive (an updraft top): the residual carries no draft mass into the cells above, and coupling them to the top draft cell would move its Newton step into them. With first-order upwinding and Courant numbers $c_w$ (residual) and $c_W$ (Jacobian), a Newton step reduces the error by about $|c_w - c_W| / (1 + c_W)$, which is below one for any $c_W \ge c_w$ but exceeds one once an updraft that accelerates within the solve outruns the Jacobian's velocity by more than $1 + c_W$. $W^j \ge w_{\mathrm{pred}} \ge w^+$ keeps each step monotone and contracting at any Courant number, as the running column maximum of the terminal velocity does in the sedimentation blocks. The buoyancy term matters for $\hat{\rho}^j$: the $(\hat{\rho}^j, u_3^j)$ block moves $c_{\Delta w} \hat{\rho}^j$ out of a cell, with $c_{\Delta w}$ the Courant number of the velocity increment of the step, which removes more than the cell holds once $c_{\Delta w} > 1 + c_W$. A $W^j$ built from the iterate alone cannot anticipate the buoyancy kick of the step, which is largest where the updraft starts from rest under a full cell.

The $u_3^j$ rows carry no buoyancy derivatives with respect to the draft scalars ($h_s^j$, $q_t^j$, condensates): each Newton step uses the buoyancy of the current iterate, which responds to the scalars at the next iteration. With those derivatives, buoyancy ($\partial u_3^j / \partial \chi^j$) and advection ($\partial \chi^j / \partial u_3^j$) form a positive feedback, in which a faster updraft advects warmer air from below and gains buoyancy. Where an almost empty draft cell sits above a much warmer one, the product of the two couplings reaches the product of the diagonals, the linearization becomes singular, and the Newton step flips sign. Lagging the buoyancy breaks the loop.

In a stably stratified draft the lagged loop has the opposite sign: a velocity error lifts air of lower $h_s^j$ from below, the buoyancy of the next iterate opposes the error, and the iterates alternate. Once the loop gain, about $(\gamma \Delta t)^2 N^2$ reduced by the damping of the $u_3^j$ and $h_s^j$ diagonals, exceeds one, as for a draft at rest under positive buoyancy, the iterates swing between rest and launch. The $u_3^j$ block therefore carries the Schur complement of the omitted $u_3^j$–$h_s^j$–buoyancy coupling, with the $h_s^j$ block replaced by its diagonal, in cells where $h_s^j$ increases from the cell below (stable_draft_mse_response, with the buoyancy per unit $h_s^j$ of draft_buoyancy_per_mse). Since a cell's upwinded $h_s^j$ responds only to the velocity at its bottom face and the face buoyancy averages two cells, each cell enters half on the diagonal of the face below it and half on the sub-diagonal of the face above it. In convecting cells the same term would shrink the diagonal, so it is left out there.

The block solver eliminates the sedimenting draft tracers first, then the draft $q_t$ and $h_s$, then $\hat{\rho}^j$, then the grid-scale condensate masses, then $\rho$ and the surface state, then $\rho q_t$, and finally the remaining scalars including $\rho e_{tot}$; the two velocity groups ($\boldsymbol{u}_h$, then $u_3^j$) are solved with a block lower-triangular solve. The automatic-differentiation algorithms (AutoSparseJacobian, AutoDenseJacobian) cover the same terms without a hand-written derivative, and the *_sparse_autodiff and *_dense_autodiff configurations use them to check the manual one.

Regularizations and state filters

Several devices keep the scheme well behaved at the edges of its validity. Each introduces a small inconsistency so that the scheme does not break down there, and each shows up in the code and in the diagnostics, so it helps to know which is which.

Environment reconstruction

Recovering a specific environment scalar from the residual requires dividing by $\hat{\rho}^0$, which vanishes as the drafts fill the cell. specific blends the residual quotient with the grid-mean quotient,

\[\psi^0 = \mathcal{W} \, \frac{\rho \psi - \sum_{j} \hat{\rho}^j \psi^j}{\hat{\rho}^0} + (1 - \mathcal{W}) \, \psi ,\]

and returns the grid-mean value outright when $\hat{\rho}^0$ falls below machine epsilon, where even a zero weight would leave a $0/0$ that automatic differentiation turns into NaNs. The weight $\mathcal{W}$ is a sigmoid in the environment area fraction $\hat{\rho}^0/\rho$ that is zero at zero area, one half at $a_{1/2}$, one above $42 a_{1/2}$, and continuously differentiable with vanishing endpoint derivatives, so the blend introduces no kinks. Where the area fraction is small, the subdomains then sum to the grid mean only approximately. $a_{1/2}$ is the a_half field of PrognosticEDMFX, which get_turbconv_model sets to the minimum draft area EDMF_min_area.

State filters

With edmfx_filter: true, enforce_edmf_updraft_constraints! runs after every timestepper stage (from constrain_state!) and, for each draft:

  • clips $\hat{\rho}^j$ to $[0, \rho^j]$ and $u_3^j$ to non-negative values, and zeroes $u_3^j$ where the face-interpolated $\hat{\rho}^j$ is negligible;
  • relaxes $h_s^j$ and $q_t^j$ to the grid-mean values where $\hat{\rho}^j$ is negligible, and otherwise bounds each draft scalar so that $\hat{\rho}^j \chi^j \le \rho \chi$;
  • rescales the draft condensate species so that their sum does not exceed $q_t^j$, mirroring the grid-mean constraint.

These are filters, not conservative corrections; the clipping of $\hat{\rho}^j$ from above and the scalar bounds are mass-conserving in the sense that the environment absorbs the difference, but the relaxation toward the grid mean is not. edmfx_filter is enabled in all PROPHET configurations in config/model_configs/ except the advection test.

Other limiters

  • The entrainment and detrainment area limiters, described in Closures, and the bounds-restoring term on $\hat{\rho}^j$ (Implicit stage for draft mass and velocity).
  • A floor of 1 m on the mixing length, which prevents division by zero in the TKE dissipation.
  • Negative TKE is relaxed to zero within one timestep rather than being clipped, in edmfx_sgs_diffusive_flux_tendency!.
  • The subgrid total-water standard deviation is bounded by $\sigma_q \le r_{\max} q_t$ (sgs_variance_max_rel_std, default 0.5), with $q_t$ the mean of the subdomain on which the quadrature is centred, before the quadrature, in set_covariance_cache!; see Closures.
  • Draft entrainment prefactors return zero at or below the surface, where $1/(z - z_s)$ is singular.

Update order within a timestep

The precomputed quantities the tendencies read are set in two passes, and the order matters because the closures depend on each other:

  1. set_implicit_precomputed_quantities!: grid-mean velocities, thermodynamics and pressure, then the draft diagnosed quantities (set_prognostic_edmf_precomputed_quantities_draft!: draft velocities, kinetic energies, temperatures, densities) and the environment residuals (..._environment!). Called before every implicit tendency evaluation, so these are consistent with the current Newton iterate. The time stepper calls set_implicit_precomputed_quantities_deferred!, which leaves the SGS moments and the implicit 1M tendency to ensure_1m_tendencies! in the Newton residual and Jacobian.
  2. set_explicit_precomputed_quantities!: surface conditions, then the PROPHET explicit closures (the entrainment rates, the surface mass-source terms; detrainment itself is assembled at each call site), then the coupled covariance/cloud-fraction Picard iteration, then the face diffusivities (which need the final cloud fraction), then the center mixing length, terminal velocities, and the microphysics tendency cache. Called before every explicit tendency evaluation.

The face diffusivities are computed after the cloud fraction because the buoyancy gradient that enters $N_e^2$ depends on it; the mixing length is materialized inside the Picard iteration when the configuration uses covariances, and separately otherwise, with uses_covariances as the shared predicate that keeps the two paths from disagreeing.
