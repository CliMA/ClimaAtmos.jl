PROPHET: Closures

The PROPHET equations leave several terms unspecified: the rates at which subdomains exchange fluid, the pressure drag between them, the eddy diffusivity that closes the intra-subdomain fluxes, the subgrid variances that the cloud and microphysics closures integrate over, and the surface boundary conditions. This page states the closures the code uses for each, and names the parameters that control them.

These closures are the parts of the scheme most likely to change. They are deliberately separated from the equations so that they can be replaced (physics-based, calibrated, or machine-learned) without touching the conservation structure. Where the implementation differs from the formulation in the PROPHET paper (Azimi et al., in preparation), that is stated.

Entrainment and detrainment

Entrainment and detrainment parameterize the exchange of mass and of the properties the exchanged fluid carries. PROPHET treats them as relaxation rates $E^{mn}$ and $\Delta^{mn}$ with units of inverse time, rather than as the fractional rates per unit length $\epsilon^{mn} = E^{mn}/w^m$ conventional in mass-flux schemes. Rates introduce no coordinate dependence through an explicit velocity. They also make plain that entrainment and detrainment relax subdomain properties toward each other, on timescales $1/E^{mn}$ and $1/\Delta^{mn}$, which makes their numerical treatment and the positivity of $\hat{\rho}^m$ tractable.

Mass and scalar conservation require the symmetry

\[\hat{\rho}^m E^{mn} = \hat{\rho}^n \Delta^{nm} ,\]

so that what subdomain $m$ gains from $n$ is what $n$ loses to $m$, and the exchange terms cancel in the sum over subdomains that recovers the resolved equations.

In the code, the total entrainment rate of draft $j$ is assembled from three pieces (compute_entrainment):

\[E^j = \varepsilon_w \, |w^j| + \varepsilon_r + \max(0, \Lambda_a) ,\]

a velocity-proportional part with an inverse-length prefactor $\varepsilon_w$, a velocity-independent rate $\varepsilon_r$, and the positive branch of a signed area-bounding rate $\Lambda_a$ (below). Two models supply $\varepsilon_w$ and $\varepsilon_r$, selected by edmfx_entr_model:

  • "Generalized" (InvZEntrainment, the default). The velocity-proportional prefactor is a fixed inverse length plus an inverse height above the surface, and the velocity-independent rate combines a buoyancy timescale with a constant relaxation rate:

    \[\varepsilon_w = \Lambda_{\max} \left( \frac{1}{L_\varepsilon} + \frac{c_\varepsilon}{z - z_s} \right) , \qquad \varepsilon_r = \Lambda_{\max} \left( c_{\varepsilon b} \, \tau_{\mathrm{buoy}}^{-1} + \tau_\varepsilon^{-1} \right) ,\]

    with $\tau_{\mathrm{buoy}}^{-1} = \min(\tau_{\max}^{-1}, (\Delta b^j)_+ / |\Delta w^j|)$ the clipped inverse buoyancy timescale built from the draft–environment buoyancy and velocity contrasts, and $\Lambda_{\max}$ the upper-area limiter.

  • "PiGroups" (PiGroupsEntrainment). The prefactor is a linear function of five nondimensional groups formed from the local subdomain state, in the manner of [42, 44]:

    \[\varepsilon_w = \frac{\Lambda_{\max}}{z - z_s} \max\!\left(0, \; \sum_{i=1}^{5} c_i |\Pi_i| + c_6 \right) ,\]

    with $\Pi_1 \propto z \, \Delta b^j / (\Delta w^j)^2$, $\Pi_2 \propto \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}} / (\Delta w^j)^2$, $\Pi_3 = \sqrt{a^j}$, $\Pi_4 = \mathrm{RH}^j - \mathrm{RH}^0$, and $\Pi_5 = z / H$ with $H$ the pressure scale height. $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$ are rescaled to $O(1)$ and clipped to $[-1, 1]$. The coefficients are entr_param_vec and are the natural target of calibration or of a learned closure.

A separate turbulent entrainment rate, which does not scale with the draft velocity and decays as the draft fills the cell,

\[E_{\mathrm{turb}}^j = \varepsilon_{t} \exp(-c_t a^j) ,\]

is added to the dynamical rate in the scalar tendencies (turbulent_entrainment; $(\varepsilon_t, c_t)$ are the two entries of turb_entr_param_vec).

Detrainment (edmfx_detr_model: "Generalized", BuoyancyVelocityDetrainment) combines a negative-buoyancy timescale with a converging mass flux:

\[\Delta^j = \max\!\left(0, \; \Lambda_{\min} \left[ c_\delta \, \tau_{\mathrm{buoy}}^{-1} - c_{\delta \nabla} \frac{\min(0, \nabla \cdot (\hat{\rho}^j \boldsymbol{w}^j))}{\hat{\rho}^j} \right] \right) + \max(0, -\Lambda_a) ,\]

with $\tau_{\mathrm{buoy}}^{-1}$ now built from the negative part of the buoyancy contrast, and $\Lambda_{\min}$ the lower-area limiter. Only a converging (negative) mass-flux divergence detrains.

Area limiters. Two mechanisms keep the draft area fraction inside $[a_{\min}, a_{\max}]$. Multiplicative limiters damp the rate that would push the area toward the bound it is approaching,

\[\Lambda_{\max} = \left[ \left(1 - \frac{a}{a_{\max}}\right)_+ \right]^{p_{\max}} \quad \text{on entrainment,} \qquad \Lambda_{\min} = \left[ \left(1 - \frac{a_{\min}}{a}\right)_+ \right]^{p_{\min}} \quad \text{on detrainment,}\]

and a signed additive rate $\Lambda_a$ (area_bounding_entr_detr) relaxes the area back if it leaves the interval at all:

\[\Lambda_a = s_{\min} \left[ \frac{(a_{\min} - a)_+}{a_{\min}} \right]^{p_{\min}} - s_{\max} \left[ \frac{(a - a_{\max})_+}{1 - a_{\max}} \right]^{p_{\max}} .\]

The two ranges are disjoint, so at most one term is nonzero. The small positive $a_{\min}$ keeps the equations from becoming singular and maintains a seed draft across convective gaps; $a_{\max}$ keeps the environment from being displaced entirely.

Departure from the formulation

In the paper, the pairwise mass flux $\hat{\rho}^m E^{mn} = \hat{\rho}^n \Delta^{nm}$ has a single shared limiter, so the symmetry relation holds by construction. The code instead treats entrainment and detrainment as unrelated closures with independent one-sided limiters, and evaluates the entrainment relaxation with $w^0$ set to zero at the call site in place of the velocity difference. Setting $w^0 = 0$ bounds the entrainment of a draft with $w^j \approx 0$, whose area would otherwise grow without limit. The analytic area solve holds the draft area fixed through the Newton iteration, which closes that path, so restoring the velocity difference is a code-side task, as is a symmetric redesign of the limiters.

Detrainment does not appear in the explicit scalar tendencies. It is absorbed into the analytic implicit solve for $\hat{\rho}^j$, and scalars are detrained implicitly through the area divergence of the mass flux; see Discretization and Time Stepping.

Pressure closure

The subgrid pressure perturbation exerts two forces on a draft: a virtual-mass effect that reduces its effective buoyancy, because accelerating displaces the surrounding fluid [52, 53], and an aerodynamic form drag [54, 55]. The formulation writes

\[\boldsymbol{d}^m = C_v (\boldsymbol{b}^m - \boldsymbol{b}) + C_d \sum_{n \ne m} \frac{\hat{\rho}^n}{\rho} \frac{\|\boldsymbol{u}^m - \boldsymbol{u}^n\|}{r^{mn}} (\boldsymbol{u}^m - \boldsymbol{u}^n) ,\]

with $r^{mn}$ the harmonic mean of subdomain length scales. Both terms satisfy the no-net-drag constraint $\sum_m \hat{\rho}^m \boldsymbol{d}^m = 0$: the virtual-mass term because the buoyancy anomalies sum to zero, the form-drag term pairwise by symmetry. The length scale shrinks with the area fraction ($r^m \propto \sqrt{a^m}$), which is necessary for well-posedness: if a draft's mass vanishes while its drag length stays finite, its relative velocity diverges [47].

The code implements this in the single-draft, environment-only setting, inside the implicit vertical-velocity solve. The virtual-mass effect appears as a buoyancy reduction factor $(1 - \alpha_b)$ with $\alpha_b$ = pressure_normalmode_buoy_coeff1, applied unconditionally. The form drag,

\[d^j = a^0 \, d_c^j \, (w^j - w^0) |w^j - w^0| , \qquad d_c^j = \frac{\alpha_d}{2 H} \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{clamp}(a^j, a_{\min}, a_{\max})}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{clamp}(a^0, 1 - a_{\max}, 1)}} \right) ,\]

with $\alpha_d$ = pressure_normalmode_drag_coeff and $H = R_d T_{s,\mathrm{ref}} / g$ a fixed reference scale height, is gated on edmfx_nh_pressure and contributes a quadratic sink to the stage equation for $w^j$. This is the pairwise form above for the draft–environment pair with $r^m = H \sqrt{a^m}$, so the harmonic mean gives the sum of $1/\sqrt{a}$ terms. The two subdomains are floored differently: the draft radius may shrink to $a_{\min}$, so $r^j \to 0$ and the drag on a vanishing draft diverges, but the environment is clamped from below at $1 - a_{\max}$. Because the vertical-velocity solve runs before the area solve, it can be handed an extrapolated $a^0$ outside $[0, 1]$, and the clamp keeps the drag finite there; with the default $a_{\max} = 0.7$ the environment factor never exceeds $1/\sqrt{0.3}$. The coefficient $d_c^j$ is computed by the function pressure_drag_coefficient for the momentum equation and for the TKE return-to-isotropy source, so the kinetic energy the drag removes from the draft is the energy the TKE budget receives. The leading $a^0$ is the pairwise weighting $\hat{\rho}^0 / \rho$ for the environment; the environment velocity is eliminated in favor of $w^j$ in the implicit solve, which brings in factors of $\rho / \hat{\rho}^0$ (see Discretization and Time Stepping).

Turbulence kinetic energy and eddy diffusivity

The grid-mean subgrid kinetic energy splits into an isotropic intra-subdomain part and a coherent inter-subdomain part,

\[\kappa_{\mathrm{SGS}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{coh}} , \qquad \kappa_{\mathrm{coh}} = \frac{1}{2\rho} \sum_{m} \hat{\rho}^m \|\boldsymbol{u}^m - \boldsymbol{u}\|^2 .\]

Only $\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}$ does the mixing, so the diffusivity and dissipation closures are functions of it alone; $\kappa_{\mathrm{coh}}$ is diagnosable from the subdomain velocities already in the state. It is $\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}$ that the model prognoses, as Y.c.ρtke (enabled by prognostic_tke).

Because the diffusive fluxes are grid-mean quantities applied uniformly across subdomains, a single grid-mean $\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}$ suffices, and the inter-subdomain transfers of isotropic TKE redistribute energy within the cell and sum to zero over it [41, 43]. Two of those transfers are nonetheless sources of grid-mean TKE, because they convert coherent subdomain kinetic energy into isotropic turbulence: the return-to-isotropy drag and the shear mixing that detrainment produces.

The budget is the compressible TKE equation [51, 56]:

\[\frac{\partial (\rho \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}})}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}} \boldsymbol{u}) = \underbrace{\nabla \cdot (\rho K_m \nabla \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}})}_{\text{transport}} + \underbrace{2 \rho K_m \|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_D\|_F^2}_{\text{shear production}} - \underbrace{\tfrac{2}{3} \rho \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}} \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{u}}_{\text{dilatation}} - \underbrace{\rho K_h \frac{\partial b}{\partial z}}_{\text{buoyancy production}} + \underbrace{\rho R_{\mathrm{coh} \to \mathrm{iso}}}_{\text{drag return-to-isotropy}} + \underbrace{\rho R_\Delta}_{\text{detrainment shear mixing}} - \underbrace{\rho D_\kappa}_{\text{dissipation}} ,\]

with $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_D$ the trace-free deviatoric strain rate of the resolved velocity. Writing the shear production as the Frobenius norm of $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_D$ makes it manifestly non-negative, as the irreversible cascade must be, and separates it from the dilatation term, which is reversible work done by macroscopic compression and expansion. The dilatation term is usually dropped under anelastic or Boussinesq assumptions; it belongs here because the dynamical core is fully compressible, and keeping it is what makes the conversion between resolved kinetic energy, internal energy, and subgrid TKE consistent.

The two coherent-to-isotropic transfers convert kinetic energy that the drafts carry as an organized vertical motion into turbulence the drafts themselves do not resolve. The pressure drag decelerates a draft relative to its environment, and the energy it removes reappears as isotropic TKE at the same rate,

\[\rho R_{\mathrm{coh} \to \mathrm{iso}} = \sum_j \hat{\rho}^j a^0 d_c^j \left| w^j - w^0 \right|^3 ,\]

with $d_c^j$ the drag coefficient of the pressure-drag closure above. Detrainment mixes air leaving a draft into the environment at a velocity contrast $w^j - w^0$, and half of the corresponding kinetic energy goes into isotropic turbulence,

\[\rho R_\Delta = \sum_j \tfrac{1}{2} \Delta^j \hat{\rho}^j \left( w^j - w^0 \right)^2 .\]

The first term is applied only when edmfx_nh_pressure is on, since it is the drag closure's counterpart; the second is always applied.

Only the diffusive part of the buoyancy flux is a source of $\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}$. The full Favre-averaged buoyancy flux is

\[\rho \overline{w' b'}^* = \underbrace{\sum_m \hat{\rho}^m (w^m - w) b^m}_{\text{coherent}} \underbrace{- \rho K_h \frac{\partial b}{\partial z}}_{\text{diffusive}} ,\]

and the coherent part drives the inter-subdomain circulation through the buoyancy term of the draft momentum equations, so it is already accounted for in $\kappa_{\mathrm{coh}}$. Including it here would double-count buoyancy production and spuriously inflate the diffusivity near cloud tops with active drafts.

Dissipation uses Taylor's surrogate,

\[D_\kappa = c_d \frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}^{3/2}}{l} ,\]

and the eddy viscosity and diffusivities are

\[K_m = c_m \, l \sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}} , \qquad K_\psi = \frac{K_m}{\mathrm{Pr}_t(\mathrm{Ri})} .\]

The turbulent Prandtl number follows [57] (with an algebraic error in the published expression corrected), as the positive root

\[\mathrm{Pr}_t = \frac{X + \sqrt{\max(X^2 - 4 \mathrm{Pr}_n \mathrm{Ri}, 0)}}{2}, \qquad X = \mathrm{Pr}_n + \omega_{\mathrm{pr}} \mathrm{Ri} ,\]

of the gradient Richardson number $\mathrm{Ri} = N_e^2 / (2 \|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_D\|_F^2)$, limited above by Pr_max. It approaches $\mathrm{Pr}_n$ in neutral conditions and grows with stability, and the stability dependence applies throughout the column rather than only in the surface layer.

Mixing-length scales

The mixing length is a smooth minimum (edmfx_scale_blending, default SmoothMinimum) of three physical scales, following [43]:

  • a wall scale $l_W = \kappa_* (z - z_s) u_* / (c_m \sqrt{e_{\mathrm{sfc}}} \, \phi_m(\zeta))$, which reproduces Monin–Obukhov similarity in the surface layer. Here $e_{\mathrm{sfc}}$ is $\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}$ in the first interior cell and $\phi_m$ is the Businger–Dyer momentum stability function of $\zeta = (z - z_s)/L$;
  • a TKE-balance scale $l_{\mathrm{TKE}} = \sqrt{c_d \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}^{3/2} / a_{pd}}$ with $a_{pd} = c_m (2\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_D\|_F^2 - N_e^2/\mathrm{Pr}_t) \sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}}$, the scale at which production balances dissipation (dropped from the blend where net production is non-positive);
  • a buoyancy scale $l_N = \sqrt{c_b \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}} / N_e$, used only in stably stratified air.

The blend is then capped by the wall distance and by the resolvability filter scale

\[\Delta_f = \max(\Delta x_h, \Delta z) ,\]

and floored at 1 m. The filter scale expresses that an eddy can be handed to the resolved dynamics only if it is resolvable in every direction, so the coarsest grid direction sets the cap. In single columns ($\Delta x_h \to \infty$) and at global-model horizontal resolutions, the cap is inert and the mixing length is purely physical, and therefore convergent under vertical refinement. In the gray zone, it binds at $\Delta x_h$, and on isotropic grids it reduces to the Deardorff bound $l \le \Delta$.

The buoyancy frequency is the moist effective one, $N_e^2 = \partial b / \partial z$, evaluated by the chain rule on the prognostic state through the saturated and unsaturated branches [58, 59], so that latent heat release across phase changes is accounted for. It is the same quantity that drives the buoyancy production in the TKE budget and enters the Richardson number, which keeps the length scale, the flux, and the budget mutually consistent.

The dissipation coefficient is derived. In stably stratified air with a buoyancy-limited mixing length, the local TKE balance is linear in $\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}$: there is no equilibrium TKE, only a threshold, and turbulence grows or decays according to the sign of $1/\mathrm{Ri} - 1/\mathrm{Pr}_t - c_d/(c_m c_b)$. As $\mathrm{Pr}_t$ grows in strong stability, that threshold approaches a critical Richardson number, so the calibratable parameter is $\mathrm{Ri}_c$ and

\[c_d = \frac{c_m c_b}{\mathrm{Ri}_c}\]

is derived from it (tke_dissipation_coefficient). The resulting basis $(c_m, c_b, \mathrm{Ri}_c)$ is nearly orthogonal: $c_m$ scales the flux magnitudes, $c_b$ partitions TKE amplitude against buoyancy length, and $\mathrm{Ri}_c$ sets the stable-regime cutoff.

Unresolved inversions

The subdomain decomposition addresses the gray zone of horizontal resolution. The vertical direction has its own gray zone, and its canonical case is the capping inversion of a cloud-topped boundary layer: the entrainment interfacial layer is meters to tens of meters thick [60], far below the $\Delta z \gtrsim 100$ m typical of global models near cloud top. A centered vertical gradient across such an interface averages the jump over a grid cell, biasing $N_e^2$ low and hence $l_N$ and $K_h$ high exactly where the stability closure should shut mixing off. The bias weakens as $\Delta z$ shrinks, so it breaks the resolution adaptivity PROPHET otherwise maintains, and its integrated effect is a slow erosion of sharp inversions and the stratocumulus decks beneath them.

Face-native evaluation. The stability pipeline is evaluated at the faces where the diffusive fluxes live. Vertical differences of cell means ($\Delta h_s$, $\Delta q_t$, and hence $\Delta b$) come from the two-point face stencil with no interpolation and no loss of sharpness; pointwise factors (thermodynamic chain-rule coefficients, $\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}$, the strain rate) vary smoothly between adjacent cells and are interpolated arithmetically. Evaluating the closure at centers and interpolating the resulting diffusivity instead cannot confine the collapse to the interface. A cell that registers the jump carries its collapsed diffusivity to both of its faces, so it damps mixing on the boundary-layer side too. Meanwhile, the interpolation across the inversion hands about half the boundary-layer diffusivity to the face where the flux should vanish.

Variances and cloud fraction

Cloud fraction and microphysical rates are strongly nonlinear in temperature and humidity, so evaluating them at the subdomain mean state biases them. PROPHET instead integrates them over an assumed subgrid distribution of $(T, q_t)$ within each subdomain, by Gauss–Hermite quadrature.

The distribution's spread comes from a diagnostic closure. Earlier EDMF formulations carried the environmental covariance prognostically [42], with its own entrainment, detrainment, and source terms; PROPHET instead diagnoses it from the resolved gradients. A subgrid parcel is displaced by a Gaussian $\boldsymbol{\delta}_\psi$ with covariance $\mathbf{D}_\psi = \mathrm{diag}(\ell_{h,\psi}^2, \ell_{h,\psi}^2, \ell_{v,\psi}^2)$ and keeps its $\theta_{li}$ and $q_t$, so the variances and cross-covariance of the two scalars at a point are

\[\Sigma_{\psi \psi} = \ell_{h,\psi}^2 \, |\nabla_h \psi|^2 + \ell_{v,\psi}^2 \, (\partial_z \psi)^2 , \qquad \Sigma_{\theta_{li} q_t} = r_{h,\theta q} \, \ell_{h,\theta} \ell_{h,q} \, \nabla_h \theta_{li} \cdot \nabla_h q_t + \ell_{v,\theta} \ell_{v,q} \, \partial_z \theta_{li} \, \partial_z q_t ,\]

with $\psi \in \{\theta_{li}, q_t\}$. The moisture horizontal scale is set by the grid, $\ell_{h,q} = \ell_{h,0} = \sqrt{c_g} \, c_{\Delta x} \Delta x_h$, with $c_g = 1/12$ the variance of a linear field over a uniform cell and $c_{\Delta x}$ (sgs_variance_horizontal_scale_factor) the scale factor, and the potential-temperature horizontal scale is $\ell_{h,\theta} = r_{\Delta x,\theta} \ell_{h,0}$ (sgs_variance_theta_horizontal_scale_ratio), with horizontal displacement cross-correlation $r_{h,\theta q}$ (sgs_variance_horizontal_cross_correlation), which lies in $[-1, 1]$. It is the variance a cell holds because the resolved field varies across it, and it vanishes as the horizontal grid is refined.

The moisture vertical scale is a displacement length, $\ell_{v,q}^2 = \ell_{v,0}^2 = 2 c_\sigma l_d^2$, with potential-temperature vertical scale $\ell_{v,\theta} = r_{v,\theta} \ell_{v,0}$ (sgs_variance_theta_vertical_scale_ratio). Here, $l_d = \min(\max(l_{\mathrm{disp}}, l_b), z - z_s)$ takes the larger of the buoyancy-bounded mixing length $l_{\mathrm{disp}} = l / \sqrt{1 + 2 c_\sigma \max(N_{\mathrm{disp}}^2, 0) \, l^2 / (c_b \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}})}$ ($l_{\mathrm{disp}} = l$ without TKE) and the floor displacement scale

\[l_b = \min\!\left( \sqrt{c_b \kappa_{\min}} \, \min\!\left(\tau_N, \dfrac{l_{\mathrm{disp}}}{\sqrt{c_b \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}} / (2 c_\sigma)}}, \tau_0\right), \; z - z_s, \; \Delta z \right) , \qquad \tau_N = \begin{cases} 1/N_{\mathrm{disp}} , & N_{\mathrm{disp}}^2 > 0 , \\[4pt] \infty , & N_{\mathrm{disp}}^2 \le 0 , \end{cases} \qquad \kappa_{\min} = \frac{w_{\min}^2}{2 c_\sigma c_b} ,\]

capped by the wall distance, with $N_{\mathrm{disp}}^2$ the larger of the dry buoyancy gradients on the cell's lower and upper bounding faces. Evaluating $N_{\mathrm{disp}}^2$ on the bounding faces rather than on the Van Leer harmonic-mean center gradients keeps the buoyancy barrier active in a cell immediately below a sharp capping inversion, and using the dry buoyancy coefficients keeps the moist latent-heat reduction at cloud base from weakening the stratification barrier. In neutral turbulent layers, $l_{\mathrm{disp}} = l$ sets $l_d$ and the vertical displacement variance $\ell_{v,0}^2 = 2 c_\sigma l^2$ follows the production–dissipation balance of [61, 62], while in stably stratified layers $\ell_{v,0}^2 = 2 c_\sigma l_{\mathrm{disp}}^2$ is smoothly bounded by the adiabatic buoyancy excursion $c_b \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}} / N_{\mathrm{disp}}^2$. The floor is the displacement of unresolved vertical motions with velocity scale $w_{\min}$: gravity waves, mesoscale circulations and convective remnants. The displacement lasts until the parcel's vertical velocity decorrelates. In stably stratified air buoyancy stops it after a time $1/N_{\mathrm{disp}}$, giving $\ell_{v,0} = w_{\min}/N_{\mathrm{disp}}$. In turbulent air the turbulence scrambles it after its Lagrangian time scale $l_{\mathrm{disp}}/\sqrt{c_b \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}} / (2 c_\sigma)}$; this approaches $1/N_{\mathrm{disp}}$ where the mixing length exceeds the buoyancy scale and reduces to the turnover time of the wall or TKE-balance scale where those are smaller, so it matches the $1/N_{\mathrm{disp}}$ limit in strongly stratified air and shortens the displacement where the mixing length is constrained below the buoyancy scale. In weakly stratified quiescent air, where $1/N_{\mathrm{disp}}$ exceeds $\tau_0$ (sgs_variance_floor_time_scale) and turbulence is weak, the displacement persists for $\tau_0$, giving $\ell_{v,0} = w_{\min} \tau_0$; where the air is neutral or unstable ($N_{\mathrm{disp}}^2 \le 0$) buoyancy imposes no limit and the same $\min(\tau_{\mathrm{turb}}, \tau_0)$ applies, so $l_b$ is continuous across $N_{\mathrm{disp}}^2 = 0$. This is the vertical dispersion of a stochastic Lagrangian model in stratified flow. The cap by the cell depth applies to the floor scale only, and the wall distance bounds $l_d$ as a whole; $l_d$ exceeds $\Delta z$ wherever $l_{\mathrm{disp}}$ does. A reversible displacement creates a fluctuation at a fixed point, and the fluctuation condenses if it crosses saturation, so the floor enters the variance and not the diffusivity, which keeps the unfloored mixing length.

The velocity scale grows with the horizontal grid scale,

\[w_{\min} = w_{\mathrm{ref}} \left( \frac{\Delta x_h}{\Delta x_{\mathrm{ref}}} \right)^p ,\]

(sgs_variance_vertical_velocity_floor, sgs_variance_floor_reference_resolution, sgs_variance_floor_resolution_exponent; a single column has no horizontal grid scale and uses $\Delta x_{\mathrm{ref}}$). The exponent follows from the spectral slope of the displaced fields. For a spectrum $E(k) \propto k^{-s}$, the variance below the grid scale is $\int_{k > 2\pi/\Delta} E \, dk \propto \Delta^{s-1}$. The horizontal term $\ell_h^2 |\nabla_h \psi|^2$ scales this way by itself: the variance of the coarse-grained gradient is $\int_{k < 2\pi/\Delta} k^2 E \, dk \propto \Delta^{s-3}$, and $\ell_h^2 \propto \Delta^2$, so $c_{\Delta x}$ is independent of resolution for any $s < 3$. The vertical term $\ell_v^2 (\partial_z \psi)^2$ does not: the vertical gradient is a vertical coarse-graining and does not change with $\Delta x_h$, so the growth of the sub-cell variance has to be carried by the amplitude, $\ell_v \propto w_{\min} \propto \Delta x_h^{(s-1)/2}$. A $-5/3$ spectrum gives $p = 1/3$. The code defaults $c_{\Delta x} = 1.24$, $w_{\mathrm{ref}} = 1.08$ m s⁻¹, $r_{\Delta x,\theta} = 0.88$, $r_{v,\theta} = 0.38$, $r_{h,\theta q} = 0.74$ and $\tau_0 = 300$ s are a fit to the within-cell moments ($\overline{q_t'^2}$, $\overline{\theta_{li}'^2}$ and their covariance) of 3.75 km global simulations (MPAS, DYAMOND) aggregated onto the model's own cubed-sphere nodes, with every closure input computed by the model's operators (the spectral-element horizontal gradient invariant, the Van Leer vertical gradient and $N^2_{\mathrm{disp}}$) on the MPAS native levels, and with the turbulent term from the model's own TKE climatology. $c_{\Delta x}$ multiplies a specific discrete gradient, so it is only meaningful with that operator. One set fits ne16 to ne60 and both the 130- and 63-level vertical grids within 1 % in cost of the per-grid fits. $\tau_0$ is bounded only from below ($\gtrsim 100$ s): in the stratified free troposphere $1/N_{\mathrm{disp}}$ binds first. The exponent is held at $p = 2/3$, which joint fits across 60, 120 and 240 km give (the two resolution pairs give the same exponent): an effective slope near $-7/3$, between the mesoscale $-5/3$ and the synoptic $-3$, which is where the spectra of temperature and humidity lie at those scales. In the large-eddy limit $w_{\min}$ vanishes and the TKE closure carries the variance.

The cross covariance follows from the same pair of displacements: the horizontal displacements of $\theta_{li}$ and $q_t$ are correlated with $r_{h,\theta q}$ and their vertical displacements are perfectly correlated, so the joint displacement covariance is positive semi-definite for $|r_{h,\theta q}| \le 1$ and the sampled correlation is

\[r = \frac{\Sigma_{T q_t}}{\sqrt{\Sigma_{TT} \Sigma_{q_t q_t}}} \qquad (\texttt{tq\_correlation\_model: diagnosed}, \text{default}),\]

after the thermodynamic Jacobian transform to the $T$ basis. It lies in $[-1, 1]$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, and its sign is set by the gradients: a parcel displaced upward in stably stratified air that is moist below arrives colder and moister, so $r < 0$ through most of the free troposphere. Where the variances vanish the diagnosed value falls back to the prescribed Tq_correlation_coefficient, which tq_correlation_model: constant uses everywhere.

The total-water standard deviation is then bounded by $\sigma_q \le r_{\max} q_t$ ($r_{\max} = 0.5$), because a wider Gaussian places a quadrature node at negative total water, and condensing the clamped excess can drive the grid-mean vapor negative. The σq bound rescales ``\Sigma{T q_t}`` by the same factor so the diagnosed correlation is invariant under the clamp.

The total grid-mean subgrid covariance adds the inter-subdomain spread to this intra-subdomain part [40, 63],

\[\Sigma_{\psi \phi}^{\mathrm{tot}} = \Sigma_{\psi \phi}^{\mathrm{iso}} + \sum_{m} \frac{\hat{\rho}^m}{\rho} (\psi^m - \psi)(\phi^m - \phi) ,\]

so that draft–environment asymmetry is carried by the spread while the isotropic piece supplies a uniform background. Because the coherent contrast is assigned to the spread, $c_\sigma$ is the intra-subdomain coefficient and is correspondingly smaller than an effective grid-scale value.

Given the mean state and this covariance, cloud fraction and microphysics are driven from a single reconstruction of the local condensate over the quadrature points, with a Lagrange multiplier chosen so that the quadrature mean reproduces the prognostic mean condensate exactly. The prognostic equations carry non-equilibrium condensate (supersaturation not yet condensed at the resolved scale, persistent supercooled liquid), while fluctuations within a subdomain are assumed to relax onto the saturation curve [61, 62, 64]. Each species' share of the reconstructed excess can be blended with its subdomain mean through the parameters sgs_liquid_uniform_fraction and sgs_ice_uniform_fraction. The equilibrium condensate the reconstruction relaxes toward is the expected saturation excess $\mathbb{E}[\max(0, S)]$ of the Gaussian, or, with sgs_variance_curvature: true, of the vertical displacement acting on the curved profile of the saturation excess. To second order in the displacement $\delta = \ell_v x$,

\[S = \mu_S + \ell_v \, \partial_z \tilde S \; x + \tfrac{1}{2} \ell_v^2 \, \partial_z^2 \tilde S \; (x^2 - 1) + h ,\]

with $\partial_z \tilde S = \partial_z q_t - r_{v,\theta}\, (\partial q_{\mathrm{sat}}/\partial T) (\partial T/\partial \theta_{li})\, \partial_z \theta_{li}$ (and likewise $\partial_z^2 \tilde S$), i.e. the saturation excess of a displaced parcel with the $\theta_{li}$ displacement scaled by $r_{v,\theta}$ as in the covariance, $x$ standard normal and $h$ the Gaussian horizontal part. The second derivative comes from the two bounding face gradients of $q_t$ and $\theta_{li}$, so the closure adds no parameter. Where the mean saturation excess has a maximum in the column, as at cloud base, the curvature is negative and the quadratic bounds $S$ by the largest excess the displaced profile reaches; the moist tail is then thinner than Gaussian and the condensate stops growing with $\sigma_S$. The expectation is the truncated-Gaussian value plus the change the curvature makes to it, with the vertical model in closed form (displacement_condensate). The cloud fraction is then the fraction of the distribution carrying positive local condensate, evaluated with a variance augmented by a non-equilibrium floor that scales with the saturation specific humidity and is released as the subdomain mean saturates. That floor keeps the cloud fraction from being driven to one by a vanishing equilibrium variance, and loosely parallels the critical-relative-humidity scaling of statistical cloud schemes [65]. Microphysical rates are evaluated at each quadrature point and aggregated linearly, which preserves total water and total energy at the subdomain level because the bulk rates preserve them pointwise.

Because the variance depends on the mixing length, the mixing length on the buoyancy gradient, and the buoyancy gradient on the cloud fraction, the closure is solved as a fixed point at each timestep. Two Picard iterations are followed by a guarded Aitken $\Delta^2$ extrapolation, which is applied only when successive increments change sign, so that the accelerated value lies between computed iterates.

The grid-mean cloud fraction is the volume-fraction-weighted sum $f_c^{\mathrm{tot}} = \sum_m a^m f_c^m$. In the current implementation the cloud fraction is a single-domain quantity: the environment's PDF inversion is applied to the grid-mean cloud condensate, $f_c = f_c^0\!\left(a^0 q_c^0 + \sum_j a^j q_c^j\right)$, with no area weighting and no separate draft term.

Under active development

The cloud-fraction and SGS-microphysics closures are under active development; the phase partition, the treatment of skewness, and the floor parameters in particular. This page deliberately states only their structure. The current forms live in cloud_fraction.jl and sgs_quadrature.jl, with the microphysics coupling in Microphysics; the configuration keys are cloud_model, use_sgs_quadrature, sgs_distribution, quadrature_order, tq_correlation_model (default diagnosed), and sgs_variance_curvature (default false).

Boundary conditions

Vertical velocity. The rigid lower boundary gives every subdomain the same Dirichlet condition as the grid mean, $w^m(z_s) = w(z_s)$, which vanishes over flat terrain. Draft velocities at interior faces then develop from buoyancy in the lowest cell. The model top is likewise rigid for all subdomains.

Draft area. No Dirichlet condition is imposed. Because the vertical velocity vanishes at the surface, the advective mass flux through the bottom face is identically zero, and the lowest cell is treated as an interior cell whose area evolves prognostically, following [44]. Drafts are instead generated by a volumetric mass source in that cell.

Surface mass source. The source injects the most buoyant fraction of surface-layer air into the draft. Its magnitude is

\[F_{\mathrm{sfc}} = a_s \, \rho \, w_* , \qquad a_s = a_{s,\max} \frac{w_*^3}{w_*^3 + c_u u_*^3} , \qquad w_* = \left[ \left( z_i \overline{w'b'}_s \right)_+ \right]^{1/3} ,\]

with $w_*$ the Deardorff convective velocity scale, $z_i$ a prescribed boundary-layer depth (convective_zi), and $a_s$ a diagnostic estimate of the surface-layer area occupied by active thermals that interpolates between free convection ($a_s \to a_{s,\max}$) and shear-dominated conditions ($a_s \to 0$). It vanishes in stable boundary layers. This closure is resolution-aware in the same sense as the mass-flux fluxes: once the grid resolves boundary-layer thermals, the resolved state carries the anomalies and the seeding shuts off.

The injected air carries the conditional mean of the upper $a_s$ tail of the surface-layer distribution,

\[\psi^{\mathrm{entr}}(z_0) = \psi(z_0) + D_{\mathcal{P}}(a_s) \, \sigma_{\psi,s} ,\]

where $\sigma_{\psi,s}$ is the surface-layer standard deviation from Monin–Obukhov similarity theory [3, 41, 42] and $D_{\mathcal{P}}(a_s)$ is the conditional mean of the distribution $\mathcal{P}$ over its top $a_s$ fraction; for a standard Gaussian, $D_{\mathcal{P}} = \phi(z_*)/a_s$ with $z_* = \Phi^{-1}(1 - a_s)$. Surface buoyancy fluctuations are observed to be positively skewed in the convective boundary layer [66], and the form above accommodates any $\mathcal{P}$; the implementation is Gaussian-only. Together with the flux boundary condition below, this reproduces the phenomenology that organized drafts are seeded by anomalously buoyant near-surface parcels while the surface flux supplies the bulk forcing.

The mass source is capped so that the drafts cannot transport more than a fraction $\alpha$ = sfc_mass_flux_cap_fraction of any surface scalar flux, leaving at least $1 - \alpha$ to the environment:

\[F_{\mathrm{sfc}} \le \alpha \, \frac{\rho \overline{w' \chi'}|_s}{\chi^{\mathrm{entr}} - \chi^0} \quad \text{for } \chi \in \{h_s, q_t\} .\]

Scalars. Surface forcing on the draft scalars is a flux condition, not a prescribed value: the same Monin–Obukhov surface flux that forces the grid-mean scalar forces every subdomain scalar,

\[\rho \, \hat{\boldsymbol{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_\psi^{\mathrm{diff}} \big|_{z_s} = \rho \overline{w' \psi'} \big|_s , \qquad \psi \in \{h_s, q_t\} ,\]

with $\rho \overline{w' q_t'}|_s$ the evaporation rate and $\rho \overline{w' h_s'}|_s$ the sum of the sensible and latent heat fluxes (under $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{h_s}^{\mathrm{diff}} \approx \boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_h^{\mathrm{diff}}$). Draft–environment thermodynamic contrasts at the surface are therefore not prescribed; they develop from the seeding and the buoyant production of vertical velocity. Diffusive surface fluxes of condensate and precipitation species vanish, mirroring the grid-mean treatment: surface-layer turbulence is not assumed to produce systematic phase imbalances between subdomains. See Surface Conditions for the bulk exchange laws.

Turbulence kinetic energy. A flux condition motivated by surface-layer scaling of the TKE budget,

\[\rho \overline{w' \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}'} \big|_{z_s} = c_k \rho u_*^3 ,\]

with $c_k$ = tke_surf_flux_coeff of order one. This is the surface-layer limit of the more elaborate condition of [43, 44], and requires the first interior level to lie within the surface layer.
