PROPHET: Horizontal Diffusion

The PROPHET (EDMFX in code) subgrid-scale (SGS) diffusive closure is, by default, vertical only: it parameterizes turbulent transport along the column with an eddy-diffusivity closure and applies the resulting tendency to the grid mean (and, under edmfx_vertical_diffusion, diffuses the prognostic updraft scalars down their own vertical gradients). Two opt-in configuration options add the analogous horizontal down-gradient terms, both defaulting to false:

  • edmfx_sgs_horizontal_diffusive_flux adds the horizontal component of the grid-mean SGS diffusive flux;
  • edmfx_horizontal_diffusion diffuses the prognostic EDMFX updraft scalars down their own horizontal gradients, and requires edmfx_sgs_horizontal_diffusive_flux.

Both terms are always explicit: they are applied in the explicit remainder tendency, independently of diff_mode. Each requires a horizontal discretization, so both return immediately on single columns.

Horizontal eddy diffusivity

The horizontal eddy viscosity and diffusivity use the same TKE-based closure as the vertical flux,

\[K_{u,h} = c_m \, l_h \, \sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}}, \qquad K_{h,h} = K_{u,h} \,/\, \mathrm{Pr}_t ,\]

where $l_h$ is the horizontal mixing length (see Mixing length), $\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}$ is the isotropic subgrid turbulent kinetic energy (Y.c.ρtke, written $e$ in some of the code comments), $c_m$ is the eddy-diffusivity coefficient, and $\mathrm{Pr}_t$ the turbulent Prandtl number. set_horizontal_diffusivities! evaluates $K_{u,h}$ and $K_{h,h}$ at cell centers on each update of the explicit precomputed cache, storing them in ᶜK_u_h and ᶜK_h_h. The horizontal tendencies and the edth/evuh diagnostics read these cached fields.

Grid-mean flux

The horizontal enthalpy and species fluxes mirror the vertical closure (Diffusion): the enthalpy bracket is

\[\mathcal{B}_h(T, q_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{eff}}, q_{\mathrm{lcl}}, q_{\mathrm{icl}}) = c_p \nabla_h T + \nabla_h \Phi + (h_{\mathrm{v}} + \Phi) \, \nabla_h q_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{eff}} + (h_{\mathrm{l}} - h_{\mathrm{v}}) \, \nabla_h q_{\mathrm{lcl}} + (h_{\mathrm{i}} - h_{\mathrm{v}}) \, \nabla_h q_{\mathrm{icl}} ,\]

where $\nabla_h$ and $\nabla_h\cdot$ are the horizontal gradient and weak divergence, $c_p$ is the heat capacity of the diffusing mixture, $\Phi$ is the geopotential, the water terms are those of [1] (Eq. 33), and $q_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{eff}} = q_{\mathrm{tot}} - q_{\mathrm{rai}} - q_{\mathrm{sno}}$ is the suspended water that diffuses. Each thermodynamic coefficient is materialized into a center scratch scalar before the weak divergence, because feeding the nested expression into $\nabla_h\cdot$ exceeds GPU kernel parameter limits on extruded spaces with warped topography.

For PrognosticEDMFX, the grid-mean horizontal diffusive flux is the subdomain sum with center weights $w^0 = \hat{\rho}^0 K_{h,h}$ and $w^j = \rho f_j K_{h,h} \hat{a}^j$ ($f_j =$ edmfx_updraft_kh_diffusion_factor, included when edmfx_horizontal_diffusion is enabled, and $\hat{a}^j$ the element-wise draft area of the section on updraft horizontal diffusion):

\[\partial_t (\rho e_{\mathrm{tot}}) \mathrel{+}= \nabla_h \cdot \left[ w^0 \, \mathcal{B}_h^0 + \sum_{j=1}^{M} w^j \, \mathcal{B}_h^j \right] ,\]

\[\partial_t (\rho q_{\mathrm{tot}}) \mathrel{+}= \nabla_h \cdot \left( w^0 \, \nabla_h q_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{eff},0} + \sum_{j=1}^{M} w^j \, \nabla_h q_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mathrm{eff},j} \right) , \qquad \partial_t (\rho \chi) \mathrel{+}= \nabla_h \cdot \left( w^0 \, \nabla_h \chi^0 + \sum_{j=1}^{M} w^j \, \nabla_h \chi^j \right) .\]

For EDOnlyEDMFX, the grid mean uses $w = \rho K_{h,h}$ on the grid-mean fields.

In the non-equilibrium schemes, the suspended cloud species $q_{\mathrm{lcl}}$, $q_{\mathrm{icl}}$ and their number concentrations $n_{\mathrm{lcl}}$, $n_{\mathrm{ice}}$ diffuse down their own horizontal gradients with the same weights, matching the phase-enthalpy fluxes in $\mathcal{B}_h$. Grid-scale species that the updrafts do not carry, such as the P3 ice number and rime quantities, diffuse down their grid-mean gradients with $\rho K_{h,h}$. Rain and snow do not diffuse horizontally, as they do not diffuse in the vertical. Passive tracers diffuse with the same weights.

The aggregate water flux additionally enters the moist-air-mass tendency,

\[\partial_t \rho \mathrel{+}= \partial_t (\rho q_{\mathrm{tot}})\big|_{\mathrm{diff},h} ,\]

while the suspended cloud-species fluxes do not.

When prognostic TKE is active, the horizontal flux transports TKE and adds the horizontal shear production,

\[\partial_t (\rho \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}) \mathrel{+}= \nabla_h \cdot (\rho \, K_{u,h} \, \nabla_h \kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}) + 2 \rho \, K_{u,h} \, \mathcal{S} : \mathcal{S}_h ,\]

with $\mathcal{S}$ the full three-dimensional strain rate of the grid-mean velocity and $\mathcal{S}_h$ the strain rate built from horizontal gradients only (the tensors written $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_D$ in Turbulence kinetic energy and eddy diffusivity). The production from vertical gradients and its stencil are applied by the vertical TKE tendency.

The momentum tendency is the horizontal weak divergence of the SGS stress $\tau = -2 K_{u,h} \mathcal{S}$, with $\mathcal{S}$ the full three-dimensional strain rate of the grid-mean velocity,

\[\partial_t u_h \mathrel{-}= \frac{1}{\rho} \nabla_h \cdot (\rho \, \tau), \qquad \partial_t u_3 \mathrel{-}= \frac{1}{\rho} \nabla_h \cdot (\rho \, \tau),\]

evaluated on cell centers for the horizontal wind and on cell faces for the vertical wind. The vertical wind is included because the horizontal flux of vertical momentum $\overline{u_h' w'}$ is a covariance, which the TKE (the half-trace of the velocity covariance) does not carry, so the down-gradient stress is its only representation. The vertical flux $\overline{w' w'}$ is a variance represented by the TKE, the mass flux, and the pressure closure, so it is not applied as down-gradient diffusion of the vertical wind, and $\partial_z w$ contributes no shear production; the vertical stress divergence on the horizontal wind is handled by the vertical diffusion pathway.

The shear production is the resolved kinetic energy the stress removes, $-\tau : \nabla_h u = 2 K_{u,h} \, \mathcal{S} : \mathcal{S}_h$, so the stress and the production are energetically consistent in the continuous limit; discretely, the production uses the lumped nodal gradient at cell centers while the momentum tendency uses the weak divergence, with the face strain rate for the vertical wind. Besides the positive-definite $2 K_{u,h} \, \mathcal{S}_h : \mathcal{S}_h$, it contains the sign-indefinite cross terms $K_{u,h} \, \partial_x w \, \partial_z u$ and $K_{u,h} \, \partial_y w \, \partial_z v$ of the stress on the vertical wind. Added to the vertical shear production $K_{u,v} \, |\partial_z u_h|^2$, with $K_{u,v}$ the vertical eddy viscosity, each pair forms the quadratic form $K_{u,v} a^2 + K_{u,h} a b + K_{u,h} b^2$ in $a = \partial_z u$, $b = \partial_x w$, which is non-negative where $K_{u,v} \ge K_{u,h} / 4$. That holds up to interpolation, because the vertical mixing length is capped at $\max(\Delta x_h, \Delta z)$ and the horizontal one at $\Delta x_h$, with the same physical scales and TKE.

Updraft horizontal diffusion

The option edmfx_horizontal_diffusion switches on horizontal diffusion of the prognostic updraft variables and requires edmfx_sgs_horizontal_diffusive_flux. For each updraft $j$, the moist static energy $\mathrm{mse}^j$, the total specific humidity $q_{\mathrm{tot}}^j$, the suspended cloud species (q_lcl, q_icl, n_lcl, n_ice), and the passive SGS tracers diffuse down their own horizontal gradients:

\[\partial_t \chi^j \mathrel{+}= \frac{1}{\hat{\rho}^j} \nabla_h \cdot \left( \rho f_j K_{h,h} \, \hat{a}^j \nabla_h \chi_{\mathrm{eff}}^j \right) , \qquad \hat{a}^j = \left\langle \max(a^j, a_\epsilon)^{-4} \right\rangle_e^{-1/4} ,\]

with $q_{\mathrm{tot},\mathrm{eff}}^j = q_{\mathrm{tot}}^j - q_{\mathrm{rai}}^j - q_{\mathrm{sno}}^j$ and $\mathrm{mse}_{\mathrm{eff}}^j$ excluding non-diffusing rain and snow enthalpy, as in the vertical treatment of edmfx_vertical_diffusion. Here $a^j = \hat{\rho}^j / \rho$ is the draft area fraction, $\langle \cdot \rangle_e$ the $WJ$-weighted mean over the spectral element (element_meanₕ), and $a_\epsilon = 10^{-8}$ a floor that keeps $a_\epsilon^{-4}$ finite in single precision (ᶜupdraft_horizontal_diffusion_weight!); the division by $\hat{\rho}^j$ uses the same floor.

The power mean $\hat{a}^j$ is a smooth element-wise minimum of the draft area, the spectral-element counterpart of the harmonic face mean that bounds the vertical updraft diffusion. In the weak divergence, the tendency at a node draws on the flux at every node of its element, so a flux carrying the nodal area $a^j$, divided by the local $\hat{\rho}^j$, would amplify the intensive tendency at draft edges by the area ratio between nodes. Since $\langle a^{-4} \rangle_e \ge \omega_i a_i^{-4}$ at every node $i$, with $\omega_i$ the node's share of the element's $WJ$, $\hat{a}^j \le \omega_i^{-1/4} a_i^j$: the draft relaxes toward its horizontal neighbors at most $\omega_{\min}^{-1/4}$ times as fast as grid-mean diffusion at the same $f_j K_{h,h}$ (about 3.5 for the default fourth-order elements), whatever its area, and an element containing a draft-free node carries almost no draft flux. The grid mean receives the same flux divergence $\nabla_h \cdot (w^j \nabla_h \chi^j)$ as the draft equation before the division by $\hat{\rho}^j$, so the diffusion conserves each scalar and leaves the diagnosed environment's scalars unchanged. The area-weighted density $\hat{\rho}^j$ receives no tendency, matching edmfx_sgs_diffusive_flux_tendency!, so, as in the vertical, the moist mass that $q_t^j$ diffusion adds and the enthalpy $(h_d^j + \Phi) \nabla_h q_{t,\mathrm{eff}}^j$ of the dry air it displaces go to the environment (see Coupling to the resolved equations).

Mixing length

The eddy diffusivity depends on direction only through the mixing length: the TKE $\kappa_{\mathrm{iso}}$ is isotropic. The horizontal mixing length is the full physical mixing length $l_\text{phys}$ of the [43] closure (the smooth minimum of the wall, TKE-balance, and static-stability scales, unchanged from the vertical closure), limited from above by a grid scale. It differs from the vertical (master) mixing length only in that grid limiter: the horizontal length uses the spectral-element node scale $\Delta x_h$ (horizontal_filter_scale), whereas the vertical pipeline uses the resolvability filter scale $\Delta_f = \max(\Delta x_h, \Delta z)$ (resolvability_filter_scale),

\[l_h = \min(l_\text{phys}, \, \Delta x_h), \qquad l = \min(l_\text{phys}, \, \Delta_f).\]

Wherever $\Delta x_h \ge \Delta z$ (single columns and GCM resolutions), the two limiters coincide ($\Delta_f = \Delta x_h$), so $l_h = l$ and the horizontal diffusivity equals the one built from the master mixing length. They differ only where $\Delta z > \Delta x_h$ (gray-zone and LES aspect ratios): there $\Delta_f = \Delta z$ while the horizontal length remains limited by $\Delta x_h$, giving the shorter horizontal scale $l_h \le l$ and the ratio $l_h / l = \Delta x_h / \Delta z$ wherever $l_\text{phys}$ exceeds both spacings.

When to enable it

Horizontal EDMF diffusion is intended for configurations whose horizontal node scale $\Delta x_h$ is comparable to or smaller than the physical mixing length, so that the horizontal limiter $\min(l_\text{phys}, \Delta x_h)$ binds. This is the high-resolution and gray-zone regime, where an anisotropic SGS length scale matters.

At coarse horizontal resolution (for example global runs with $\Delta x_h$ of tens of kilometers), the horizontal limiter rarely binds, the horizontal diffusivity reduces to the isotropic environment value, and the term is typically negligible next to resolved horizontal transport. Both options are off by default for that reason.

The Smagorinsky–Lilly and anisotropic-minimum-dissipation closures (Large-Eddy Simulation Closures) already supply horizontal SGS diffusion of the same fields, so combining edmfx_sgs_horizontal_diffusive_flux with either is rejected at model construction, as is edmfx_horizontal_diffusion without edmfx_sgs_horizontal_diffusive_flux.

Because the term is explicit, it adds a horizontal diffusive stability limit on the timestep, $\Delta t \lesssim \Delta x_h^2 / (2 K_{h,h})$. At the fine horizontal resolutions where this closure is intended, the timestep is in practice already set by the explicit horizontal acoustic limit $\Delta t \lesssim \Delta x_h / c_s$, which is the more restrictive of the two.

Configuration

edmfx_sgs_horizontal_diffusive_flux: true   # default: false
edmfx_horizontal_diffusion: true            # default: false; requires the flux option

Diagnostics

Three diagnostic variables expose the horizontal closure fields:

  • lmixh: the horizontal mixing length $l_h$ [m], recomputed on demand with the grid-scale limit set by the horizontal node spacing;
  • edth: the horizontal eddy diffusivity for scalars $K_{h,h}$ [m² s⁻¹], read from the cached ᶜK_h_h;
  • evuh: the horizontal eddy viscosity $K_{u,h}$ [m² s⁻¹], read from the cached ᶜK_u_h.

The vertical counterparts are lmix, edt, and evu.
