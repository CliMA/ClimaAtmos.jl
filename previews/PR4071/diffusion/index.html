Diffusion

Diffusion in ClimaAtmos represents physical subgrid-scale turbulent transport: the vertical mixing of a boundary layer the grid does not resolve, and the horizontal mixing an eddy-viscosity closure supplies. It is distinct from Hyperdiffusion, which is a numerical filter with no physical content, though both smooth gradients and both therefore reduce the spurious oscillations and negative tracer values described in Microphysics.

This page collects what the diffusive terms act on and how they are split between the implicit and explicit halves of the timestep. The closures that supply the diffusivities are documented elsewhere: Large-Eddy Simulation Closures for the resolved-eddy closures, Turbulence kinetic energy and eddy diffusivity for the TKE-based eddy diffusivity, and the two prescribed profiles below.

Vertical diffusion

Two prescribed-diffusivity closures are available through the vert_diff configuration key. Both are boundary-layer schemes: the diffusivity is a function of height or pressure alone.

vert_diffTypeEddy diffusivity
~ (default)noneNo vertical diffusion
"VerticalDiffusion"ClimaAtmos.VerticalDiffusion$K_E = C_E |\boldsymbol{u}_a| z_a$ below the 850 hPa level, tapered above
"DecayWithHeightDiffusion"ClimaAtmos.DecayWithHeightDiffusion$K = D_0 \exp[-(z - z_s) / H]$

Here $\|\boldsymbol{u}_a\|$ and $z_a$ are the wind speed and height of the lowest model level, and $C_E$ is a dimensionless coefficient. Where the pressure falls below 850 hPa, above the boundary layer, the surface-driven diffusivity decays as

\[K_E \exp \left[ - \left( \frac{p_{pbl} - p}{p_{strato}} \right)^2 \right] , \qquad p_{pbl} = 850 \, \mathrm{hPa} , \quad p_{strato} = 100 \, \mathrm{hPa} ,\]

which confines the mixing to the boundary layer and the lower free troposphere. The same value serves as the eddy viscosity for momentum; disable_momentum_vertical_diffusion restricts either closure to the scalars.

Face diffusivities are formed as a harmonic mean of the two neighboring center values rather than an arithmetic one. The flux then collapses at a face separating a turbulent layer from quiescent, strongly stratified air, where an arithmetic mean would leave about $K/2$.

Vertical diffusion can instead come from PROPHET, whose diffusive flux is closed by a prognostic turbulence kinetic energy, or from the LES closures: a vertically acting Smagorinsky–Lilly, or AMD, which always acts on both axes. These paths are alternatives, and a configuration that sets vert_diff alongside turbconv, amd_les, or a vertically acting smagorinsky_lilly is rejected at model construction, as is edmfx_sgs_horizontal_diffusive_flux alongside an LES closure in the horizontal.

What each variable receives

Energy diffuses through the enthalpy flux

\[\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_E = -\rho K_h \left[ c_p \nabla_v T + \nabla_v \Phi + (h_v + \Phi) \nabla_v q_t^{\mathrm{eff}} + (h_l - h_v) \nabla_v q_l^{\mathrm{cl}} + (h_i - h_v) \nabla_v q_i^{\mathrm{cl}} \right] ,\]

with $q_t^{\mathrm{eff}} = q_t - q_{\mathrm{rai}} - q_{\mathrm{sno}}$ the suspended water that diffuses, $q_l^{\mathrm{cl}}$ and $q_i^{\mathrm{cl}}$ the suspended cloud liquid and cloud ice specific humidities, $h_\mu(T)$ the specific enthalpy of water phase $\mu \in \{v, l, i\}$, and

\[c_p = (1 - q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}) c_{pd} + q_v c_{pv} + q_l^{\mathrm{cl}} c_{pl} + q_i^{\mathrm{cl}} c_{pi}\]

the isobaric heat capacity of the diffusing mixture (rain and snow mass counts at $c_{pd}$; see below). The sensible-heat and geopotential terms apply in every configuration (with $c_p = c_{pd}$ in dry runs), the water terms when $\rho q_t$ is prognostic.

Departure from the formulation

The water terms are those of [1] (Eqs. 30 and 33). That formulation writes the sensible-heat term as the dry-static-energy gradient $\nabla_v s_d = c_{pd} \nabla_v T + \nabla_v \Phi$; the code uses the heat capacity $c_p$ of the diffusing mixture, which adds the heat capacity of the water carried by the eddies and makes a moist adiabat an exact fixed point of diffusion (second bullet below).

Three physical and mathematical properties determine this form:

  • Energy–species consistency. In the dry-air reference frame [5], the subgrid-scale flux of dry air is zero and each diffusing water phase $\mu \in \{v, l, i\}$ carries its total specific enthalpy $h_{\mathrm{tot},\mu} = h_\mu(T) + \Phi$, so the water-enthalpy flux is $\sum_\mu (h_\mu + \Phi) \boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{q_\mu}$ ([1], Eq. 30). Eliminating the vapor specific humidity via $q_v = q_t^{\mathrm{eff}} - q_l^{\mathrm{cl}} - q_i^{\mathrm{cl}}$ and closing each suspended phase flux down its own gradient, $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{q_\mu} = -\rho K_h \nabla_v q_\mu$, yields the water terms above: $(h_v + \Phi) \nabla_v q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the enthalpy carried by the net suspended-water flux, and $(h_l - h_v) \nabla_v q_l^{\mathrm{cl}} = -L_v(T) \nabla_v q_l^{\mathrm{cl}}$ and $(h_i - h_v) \nabla_v q_i^{\mathrm{cl}} = -L_s(T) \nabla_v q_i^{\mathrm{cl}}$ carry the latent enthalpy of condensate diffusing one way and vapor the other at fixed $q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$. The sensible-heat term is the eddy flux of enthalpy at fixed composition, $\rho c_p \overline{w'T'}$: eddies displace parcels of the diffusing mixture, and each parcel has the heat capacity of that mixture, so the mass that does not diffuse (rain and snow outside the LES closures) contributes no heat capacity to the flux. Each thermodynamic coefficient is evaluated at cell centers and interpolated to faces outside the gradient so that the flux factors into $\sum_\mu h_{\mathrm{tot},\mu} \boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{q_\mu}$ plus the sensible-heat flux. Diffusing total moist enthalpy $-\rho K_h \nabla_v (h + \Phi)$ instead would differentiate $-h_d q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ as if diffusing water displaced dry air rather than adding moist mass $\partial_t \rho = -\nabla_v \cdot (\rho \boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{q_t})$, and would differentiate $h_\mu(T)$ inside the gradient.
  • Moist-adiabatic neutrality. Turbulent eddies exchange parcels that conserve $q_t$ and the moist static energy $\tilde h + \Phi$ of the diffusing mixture during reversible adiabatic displacements, where $\nabla_v (\tilde h + \Phi) = c_p \nabla_v T + \nabla_v \Phi + (h_v - h_d) \nabla_v q_t^{\mathrm{eff}} + (h_l - h_v) \nabla_v q_l^{\mathrm{cl}} + (h_i - h_v) \nabla_v q_i^{\mathrm{cl}}$. The bracket above therefore equals $\nabla_v (\tilde h + \Phi) + (h_d + \Phi) \nabla_v q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$, and vanishes identically in a layer that is well mixed in $(\tilde h + \Phi, q_t)$: a moist adiabat, saturated or unsaturated, is a fixed point of diffusion, with temperature decreasing and condensate increasing with height while the latent phase-exchange fluxes balance the sensible-heat flux exactly. The neutral unsaturated profile is $dT/dz = -g / c_p$, the moist-air adiabatic lapse rate. With the dry-static-energy gradient $c_{pd} \nabla_v T$ in place of $c_p \nabla_v T$, a moist-adiabatic layer carries a residual flux $-(c_p - c_{pd}) \nabla_v T$ of about $1\%$ of the sensible-heat flux, directed down the temperature gradient. Weighting $\nabla_v q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ by a single phase-averaged enthalpy $h_{\mathrm{eff}} + \Phi$ (as in fourth-order Hyperdiffusion) would omit the latent phase-exchange fluxes when condensate and vapor counter-diffuse at uniform $q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$.
  • Reference-temperature invariance. Under a shift $T_0 \to T_0 + \delta T_0$ with Kirchhoff-consistent reference latent heats, all three water-phase enthalpies shift by the same constant $-c_{pl} \delta T_0$ (see Thermodynamics and the Working Fluid). The sensible-heat term depends on $T_0$ only through $T$ and the phase differences $h_l - h_v$ and $h_i - h_v$ are invariant, and the shift of $(h_v + \Phi) \nabla_v q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ cancels the shift of $\partial_t (\rho e_{\mathrm{tot}})$ through the moist-mass continuity source $\partial_t \rho = \partial_t (\rho q_t)|_{\mathrm{diff}}$ (whereas $-\rho K_h \nabla_v (h + \Phi)$ shifts by $(c_{pd} - c_{pl}) \delta T_0 \nabla_v q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$, leaving an uncompensated $c_{pd} \delta T_0 \, \partial_t (\rho q_t)|_{\mathrm{diff}}$ residual).

The heat capacity $c_p$ counts rain and snow mass at $c_{pd}$ (a $10^{-4}$ relative effect) so that it equals the coefficient of $\nabla T^j$ in the gradient of the moist static energy that the PROPHET updrafts diffuse, which makes the energy the diagnosed environment absorbs from updraft diffusion exactly the enthalpy of the displaced dry air (see Coupling to the resolved equations). In the LES closures, which diffuse precipitation as well, $c_p$ is the heat capacity of the full moist mixture.

Water diffuses through the effective total water $q_t^{\mathrm{eff}} = q_t - q_{\mathrm{rai}} - q_{\mathrm{sno}}$. The same flux increments both $\rho q_t$ and $\rho$, so diffusing water carries the corresponding moist-air mass. In the non-equilibrium schemes (1M, 2M, and 2MP3), the prognostic suspended cloud species (ρq_lcl, ρq_icl, ρn_lcl, ρn_ice, and the rime mass ρq_rim and rime volume ρb_rim that P3 cloud ice carries) diffuse down their own specific gradients with the same $\rho K_h$, matching the species fluxes $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{q_l^{\mathrm{cl}}}$ and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{F}}_{q_i^{\mathrm{cl}}}$ whose latent enthalpy the energy equation carries (add_moisture_diffusionᵥ!). Because the rime fields diffuse with the same linear operator as cloud ice, the rime mass stays within the ice mass. In the equilibrium 0-moment scheme, $q_l^{\mathrm{cl}}$ and $q_i^{\mathrm{cl}}$ are diagnostic (ᶜq_liq and ᶜq_ice) and have no separate prognostic equations.

Rain, snow, and rain number density do not diffuse under the prescribed and PROPHET closures or the viscous sponge. They evolve through sedimentation and microphysical sources; see Microphysics. The LES closures, whose subgrid eddies carry falling precipitation, diffuse them as well (see below).

Passive grid-mean tracers diffuse independently with the full $K_h$.

Momentum receives $\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\tau} / \rho$ with the stress $\boldsymbol{\tau} = 2 \rho K_u \boldsymbol{S}$ and $\boldsymbol{S}$ the vertical strain rate, under zero-flux boundary conditions. The surface stress is added by the separate surface-flux tendency, described in Surface Conditions.

Implicit or explicit

Vertical diffusion is stiff at the timesteps of interest, so implicit_diffusion: true moves the whole tendency into the implicit solve. The model's diff_mode follows that key: under Implicit() the tendency is evaluated from implicit_tendency! and linearized in the Jacobian; under Explicit() it is evaluated from additional_tendency!. The global configurations with a 60 km model top set implicit_diffusion: true, as does every PROPHET configuration except the advection test, and those pair it with approximate_linear_solve_iters: 2. See Implicit Solver.

Horizontal diffusion

Horizontal diffusion comes from an eddy-viscosity closure, never from a prescribed profile: Smagorinsky–Lilly [11], anisotropic minimum dissipation [68], a constant horizontal diffusivity, or the optional PROPHET horizontal diffusive flux. All of them are explicit. See Large-Eddy Simulation Closures and PROPHET Horizontal Diffusion.

All four horizontal closures (as well as the second-order horizontal viscous sponge) use the same enthalpy decomposition and species treatment as the vertical closures, through the shared add_scalar_diffusionₕ! for the grid mean and add_sensible_heat_diffusionₕ! and add_water_enthalpy_diffusionₕ! for the PROPHET subdomains. PROPHET horizontal diffusion and the viscous sponge diffuse $q_t^{\mathrm{eff}}$ and the suspended cloud species down their own horizontal gradients while rain and snow do not diffuse. The LES closures, horizontal and vertical (Smagorinsky–Lilly, anisotropic minimum dissipation, and constant horizontal diffusion), diffuse the total water $q_t$ and every microphysics species, rain, snow, and rain number included, down its own gradient; in the enthalpy bracket, rain enters with the liquid water and snow with the ice, $q_l = q_l^{\mathrm{cl}} + q_{\mathrm{rai}}$ and $q_i = q_i^{\mathrm{cl}} + q_{\mathrm{sno}}$ (ᶜdiffusing_water_phases with precipitation = true). Smagorinsky–Lilly, constant horizontal diffusion, PROPHET horizontal diffusion, and the viscous sponge use a single scalar diffusivity for the sensible heat and all water terms, while anisotropic minimum dissipation evaluates $D(\nabla_h T)$ for the sensible-heat and geopotential terms and $D(\nabla_h q_t)$ for the water terms and microphysics species so that each species flux and its enthalpy use the same diffusivity.
